# Research Protocol

## 1. Candidate discovery

Use at least three source classes when practical:

1. official registries, regulators, tax or corporate records;
2. sector associations, accreditation bodies, or authoritative directories;
3. official organization websites and public filings;
4. reputable sector publications, rankings, conference programs, or institutional partners.

Start with the user’s sources, then add targeted searches for missing subcategories, geography, and likely prominent organizations. Search by sector synonyms, organization type, and regional terms. Keep every discovery source in the source ledger.

## 2. Entity resolution

Confirm whether each candidate is a legal organization, brand, program, fund, chapter, affiliate, parent, or portfolio company. Use domain, address, legal identifier, official relationship language, filings, and dated announcements. When evidence is insufficient, preserve both candidates and mark the relationship `Unclear`.

## 3. Currentness

Check the official site and the newest relevant filing or announcement. Record the date checked. For inactive, acquired, renamed, or closed entities, retain the record when it was a plausible candidate and set `Operating Status` accordingly. Do not silently replace a former entity with its successor.

## 4. Quick mission / role

Write one neutral sentence, normally paraphrased from an official source. Separate the organization’s stated mission from the researcher’s interpretation. Keep it descriptive, not promotional.

## 5. Stopping rule

Stop only when every planned source class has been searched, each material subcategory/geography has candidate coverage, duplicate and exclusion review is complete, and the marginal search is producing no new plausible prominent organizations. Document queries, dates, sources checked, and exclusions. If this cannot be established, label the output `coverage-limited`, not complete.

## 6. Confidence

- `High`: identity and required facts confirmed by current primary sources.
- `Medium`: identity confirmed, but one material field depends on an authoritative secondary source or is dated.
- `Low`: plausible candidate with unresolved identity, currentness, or prominence.

Never convert low confidence into a definitive fact through inference.
